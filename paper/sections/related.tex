\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Layer-wise readouts.}
The logit lens decodes intermediate residual states with the model's own final norm and unembedding \citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens}.
It is biased in early layers, whose representations are not in the basis the unembedding expects; the tuned lens fixes this with an affine translator per layer, trained so that each layer's decoded distribution matches the final one \citep{belrose2023tuned}.
\citet{wendler-etal-2024-llamas} used the logit lens to show that Llama-2 passes through an English-like latent space when translating between other languages.
\citet{panchal-etal-2026-indic} adapt the tuned lens to ten Indian languages.
\citet{huang2026alignment} use the logit lens on long-tail factual questions and find that confident wrong answers in instruction-tuned models arise in late layers.
We use both lenses and report the tuned lens as a secondary readout.

\paragraph{Prediction depth.}
\citet{baldock2021difficulty} defined the prediction depth of an example as the first layer after which a nearest-neighbour probe agrees with the final prediction, and found that it tracks example difficulty, consistency of learning and margin.
Our crystallization depth is the analogue for a language model's multiple-choice answer, read through a lens rather than a probe.

\paragraph{Where facts are recalled.}
Causal tracing locates factual recall in middle-layer MLPs at the last subject token \citep{meng2022locating}.
\citet{geva-etal-2023-dissecting} describe recall as subject enrichment in early and middle layers followed by attribute extraction by attention at the final position.
\citet{zhang2024patching} compare activation-patching choices and recommend logit-difference metrics and same-distribution corruptions (replacing the subject with another real subject) over Gaussian noise.
Our patching follows that advice: we swap the entity for a same-attribute entity from another state and measure normalised logit-difference recovery.

\paragraph{Frequency and knowledge.}
Question-answering accuracy rises with the number of pretraining documents that mention the relevant entities \citep{kandpal2023longtail}, and with entity popularity measured by Wikipedia pageviews \citep{mallen-etal-2023-trust}.
We use both kinds of signal: exact-string counts in an open pretraining corpus, Dolma \citep{soldaini-etal-2024-dolma}, through the infini-gram engine \citep{liu2024infinigram}, and Wikipedia pageviews as a check.
These studies relate frequency to whether the answer is right; we ask whether it relates to where the answer forms when it is right.

\paragraph{Culture inside the model.}
SANSKRITI \citep{maji-etal-2025-sanskriti} evaluates knowledge of Indian culture across states, union territories and cultural attributes; we use it as released.
CultureScope \citep{zhang2025culturescope} probes cultural understanding along a set of cultural dimensions, as a benchmark; it is behavioural.
Two layer-wise studies are closest to ours.
CulTrace \citep{yu2025cultrace} trains one decoder per layer (LoRA adapters on a decoder LLM, following LatentQA) that verbalises the hidden states at the instruction tokens after a free-form cultural question, and labels each layer's text with an LLM judge.
On BLEnD and a question set built from CANDLE, ten countries each, with three instruction-tuned models, it finds a staged order (question domain, then culture, then answer) and a later first mention of the target culture for less-represented countries.
\citet{chelombitko2026cultural} probe 270 mythological entities from ten traditions, including Indian, in 18 instruction-tuned models with linear probes, the logit lens and activation patching.
They find that culture is linearly decodable well before the gold name is emitted, that the gold name enters the logit lens late (last 12\% of layers) and about 1.3 layers later for non-Greco-Roman traditions, and that patching effects peak late.
We share their question of where cultural knowledge resolves and their direction of interest, but differ in four ways.
We work within one country, comparing entities across \nStates{} states and union territories rather than ten cultures.
We measure documentation as corpus counts and test it per question with controls and a preregistered null, where both studies compare culture means against an assigned representation label.
Our depth is the settling layer of a multiple-choice answer read directly from the model, debiased for answer letters and checked for reliability, rather than the first layer at which a decoder names a culture or a gold token enters the top-$k$.
And we relate patching depth to lens depth per question rather than per model.
Other mechanistic work on culture localises where cultural information sits rather than when it resolves: sparse-autoencoder features whose ablation harms culturally conditioned tasks \citep{zou-etal-2026-deciphering}, steering vectors that close the gap between explicitly and implicitly localised answers \citep{veselovsky-etal-2026-localized}, and a few mid-layer attention heads that bind cultural items to identities \citep{floro2026binding}.
In a search of arXiv and the ACL Anthology in October 2026 we found no work that studies SANSKRITI or another Indian cultural benchmark mechanistically.
